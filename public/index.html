The usual way

Fifteen minutes, a portal message, and a referral to someone you have never met.

Most primary care runs on volume. It is not that doctors stop caring. A schedule with thirty patients a day leaves no room to listen, to call you back, or to notice what changed since last year.

  1. 01

    Weeks to get in

    By the time there is an opening, you have either recovered or ended up at urgent care.

  2. 02

    Nobody who knows the whole picture

    Five specialists, five portals, and no one doctor who has read all of it.

  3. 03

    Questions that wait

    A message at 7 p.m. gets an answer in two days, from whoever is covering.

Your physician

One physician who knows your history, and has the time to use it.

Dr. Shtern trained in internal medicine and then in nephrology, and is board-certified in both. That combination matters more than it sounds: kidney function and blood pressure shape almost every prescription an internist writes.

She started Legacy Medical so she could take on fewer patients and do more for each of them. You see her at every visit, and members can reach the practice by phone at any hour.

Membership

What membership includes

An annual membership fee covers access to Dr. Shtern. Office visits and tests are billed to your insurance, as they are now.

  • Same or next-business-day visits

    When something comes up, you are seen that day or the next business day, not in three weeks.

  • Phone access around the clock

    Members can reach the practice by phone 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including weekends and holidays.

  • Extended visits

    Appointments are long enough to cover everything on your list, with minimal time in the waiting room.

  • The annual Legacy physical

    A thorough yearly exam with lab work and an ECG, then time to go through the results together.

  • Lab draws in the office

    Blood is drawn here, so there is no separate trip to a lab. Stool occult blood testing too.

  • ECGs in the office

    Electrocardiograms done during your visit, without a separate appointment elsewhere.

  • Rapid respiratory tests

    In-office testing for flu A and B, COVID-19 and strep, so a sick visit ends with an answer.

  • Telehealth

    Video visits when coming in is not practical, including for many follow-ups.

  • Specialist coordination

    Dr. Shtern picks the specialist, makes the call, and keeps track of what they find.

Why it matters

Small changes are easier to catch early.

A creatinine that creeps up a little each year, blood pressure that is fine in the office and high at home, a new medicine that does not sit well with an old one. In a rushed system these slip between visits.

With a doctor who knows your baseline, they come up in conversation instead of in the emergency room.
